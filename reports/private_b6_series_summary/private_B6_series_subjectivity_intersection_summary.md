# Private B6 Series: Subjectivity Intersection Exploratory Audit

Status: exploratory/private result bundle. Generated from local B6 audits.

## Purpose

B6 was separated from the C12-centered Stage B5 line as an exploratory audit of the ontology of Subjectivity Intersection. The main question was not whether C improves C12 directly, but whether C-mediated access behaves differently from objectification, direct A-B readout, and substituted C controls.

## Main Thread

- B6E/B6K: C-state carries adaptive operator-selection information, with a GMR72/FES bridge adding a useful operational bridge.
- B6F/B6FR: recursive self-updating of future C was not supported.
- B6G/B6H: bidirectional perspectival transport and C-conditioned coordinate transform were not supported.
- B6N: scoring subject-position as an object was weak.
- B6O/B6P: intersection access survived more than objectification.
- B6U: objectification failed across all final objectification conditions; intersection access survived only in supported regimes.
- B6V: direct A-B access without C failed in all tested conditions, while C-mediated access survived in supported regimes.
- B6W corrected: true C was partially non-substitutable against shuffled/random/balanced/foreign/phase-rotated C controls.
- B6Y/B6Z corrected: broad C-basin was not supported; phase-dependent C access often collapsed under small phase displacement.

## Corrected Key Results

### B6U: Objectification Failure

- final objectification failure: 24 / 24 direction conditions
- intersection survival: 13 / 24 direction conditions
- objectification failure with intersection survival: 13 / 24 direction conditions

Interpretation: subjectivity-like structure was not supported as an object score, but operational access-by-intersection survived in specific regimes.

### B6V: Access Without C

- no-C direct A-B failure: 24 / 24
- with-C intersection survival: 15 / 24
- C-necessary pattern: 15 / 24

Interpretation: the surviving access effect was not reproduced by direct A-B access alone.

### B6W Corrected: C Substitution

- true-C non-substitutable conditions: 11 / 24
- mean substitution pass count: 2.833 / 5

Interpretation: true C was not freely substitutable. The earlier phase-neighbor-tolerant interpretation was withdrawn after fixing phase=qN rotation handling.

### B6Y/B6Z Corrected: Phase Collapse

B6Y corrected:

- broad C-basin was not supported.
- many phase-dependent regimes collapsed by about 45 degrees.

B6Z:

- phase-collapse detected: 15 / 24
- narrow boundary <=45 degrees: 15 / 24
- median first collapse: 22.5 degrees
- mean tolerance width: 33.75 degrees

Interpretation: in the supported phase-dependent regimes, C-mediated intersection access is phase-sensitive, often collapsing at approximately 22.5 degrees.

## Current Safe Claim

The B6 exploratory series supports the following limited claim:

Objectification fails, direct A-B access without C fails, and the surviving access effect is concentrated in C-mediated intersection regimes. Corrected substitution and phase-collapse audits indicate that the C-mediated access condition is not freely substitutable and is often phase-sensitive.

This does not establish C as an observed object, AGI, consciousness, qualia, or recursive self-improvement.

## GMR72 Connection

B6K/B6O-S/B6O-T suggest that GMR72-style structured phase bridging is relevant to the supported intersection-access pathway. The strongest safe interpretation is that structured GMR phase bridging contributes to access operations, while true72-only specificity is not yet established.

This is the main point where Luke's research perspective may be especially useful.
